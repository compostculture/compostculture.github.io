Methodology

How we evaluate gear.

Every roundup on this site runs the same process, from candidate selection to the verdict paragraph. This page is that process, written down — including the parts that are weaker than an honest page should admit.

The bar, in the open · Updated November 2026

First, what “tested” does not mean here

No product on this site has been through a hands-on lab test yet, and the site never claims otherwise — no invented measurements, no fake field notes. What every verdict actually rests on:

  • Published specifications — manufacturer-stated dimensions, materials, capacities, and warranties, read against each other rather than against marketing copy.
  • Long-term user reports — multi-season accounts from composting forums, group-buy threads, and marketplace review histories. This is the closest available substitute for a two-year hands-on test, and it is exactly where cheap gear shows its failure modes: snapped thermometer stems, cracked trays, lids that stop sealing.

Where a page leans on one source type more than the other, the page says so in its table notes — for example, every spec figure on the worm-bin pages was re-verified against live listings on November 6, 2026. Hands-on testing is on the roadmap; when a product has been run in the field, the verdict will carry the method, the duration, and the dates.

How products earn a seat at the table

Candidates come from the gear readers actually ask about: the models that dominate composting forums, group-buy threads, and marketplace best-seller lists. A product has to clear three checks before it makes a comparison table: published specs complete enough to compare, enough long-term user reports that failure modes are visible, and a real job it plausibly does well. Products that fail any of the three are excluded, and exclusions are stated on the page rather than left invisible — several roundups carry an explicit “the anti-list” of gear that doesn’t earn its shelf space.

Lists stay short on purpose: five to seven picks, the way we’d actually shop. Volume is not a service — a 30-item list outsources the decision instead of making it.

The comparison bar

Each category gets its own criteria table, set before the products and never bent to fit a verdict. The recurring principle: judge the physics of the job, not the marketing of the product. A worm bin is judged on four problems — air, moisture, drainage, harvesting. A thermometer on hermetic sealing, stem gauge, and published accuracy. A tumbler on aeration access, lid seal, and flip weight. A countertop machine on drum volume, daily throughput, and the consumable cost its marketing leaves out. The exact criteria live in each page’s “How we pick” section, which is written against that page’s actual table columns.

Awards follow the bar, not the price of the slot: every roundup names one Best overall (the pick-01 badge) plus category winners that describe a situation — best budget, best for a large household, best European option. A product’s rank and its commission are decided by different processes that never touch.

Prices, specs, and dates

Prices move weekly, so they appear as tiers ($ to $$$+) with the legend printed beside each table, plus the manufacturer-stamped prices where a vendor publishes them — each with its verification date. Specs are re-checked against live listings when a page is revised, and the revision date rides in the table notes. Photography is Wikimedia Commons (CC-licensed, credited on-page and in the credits record); manufacturer renders appear only where the vendor publishes them.

Affiliate partnerships and independence

The site is supported by affiliate links — Amazon for gear, Hungry Worms for live worms, GEME Bio for the countertop composters, and Jackery, BLUETTI, Renogy and UDPOWER for portable power. Every affiliate link is labeled as one, on every page, and the full disclosure sits in every footer and on the About page.

The independence rules, stated plainly: no sponsored placements, no paid rankings, no product enters a list because a program pays well, and commission rates never enter a verdict. The evidence bar is the same for a partner product as for a product with no affiliate program at all — which is why some popular products appear here as exclusions. Recommendations are small on purpose: five to seven picks, only what we’d actually buy.

Corrections

If a spec, number, or characterization on this site is wrong, email corrections@compostculture.com with the page and what you believe is true — including your own long-term experience with a product. Corrections get verified against the source of record, the page changes, and the verified date updates. Products and first-hand reports readers volunteer feed directly into future roundups.

What every number points to

Four source types exist on this site, and they are never blended silently:

  1. Manufacturer specs

    Listed dimensions, materials, capacities, warranties — vendor-published, verified against live listings on a stated date.

  2. Published research & agency guidance

    Temperature thresholds (EPA sanitization guidance), C:N ranges, vermicomposting literature — the figures the science articles cite.

  3. Long-term user reports

    Forum threads, group-buy experience, marketplace review histories over multiple seasons — qualitative by necessity, never dressed up as a lab result.

  4. Our own arithmetic

    Multi-year cost comparisons and capacity math built from the verified specs above — assumptions shown, so you can rebuild the number yourself.
