Why measurement

A compost pile is a reactor with a temperature curve, an oxygen budget, and a clock. The mesophilic bacteria warm it to roughly 120°F, the thermophiles push it past 131°F — the threshold where the EPA requires three days of sustained heat to sanitize pathogens — and then the pile cools into curing. You can run all of that blind, and you probably will for a while. But the moment you want finished compost on a schedule, or weed-free and pathogen-low output for vegetable beds, you need instruments and equipment matched to the job.
